\subsection{Algorithm engineering}\label{sec:engineering}

The search described above spends essentially all of its time in one place, and
saying exactly where turns out to determine what is worth optimising.

\subsubsection*{Profile}

Instrumenting each phase of the solver with \texttt{CLOCK\_THREAD\_CPUTIME\_ID}
on three representative cells --- $\Kq{6}{6}{3}$ at $M=41$ ($q^n=46\,656$),
$\Kq{6}{8}{4}$ at $M=169$ ($q^n=1\,679\,616$) and $\Kq{8}{9}{4}$ at $M=2944$
($q^n=134\,217\,728$) --- gives candidate evaluation $92.9\%$, $98.5\%$ and
$99.6\%$ of the run time respectively. Committing a move takes $3.3\%$,
$0.8\%$ and $0.4\%$; choosing the uncovered word, computing the $M$ Hamming
distances and breaking ties together account for under $2\%$ everywhere.
The cost of one evaluated sphere pattern grows from $1.9$\,ns to $6.0$\,ns to
$7.4$\,ns as the counter array grows from $93$\,kB (L2-resident) to $3.4$\,MB
(L3) to $268$\,MB (DRAM): the walk is a strided gather and its throughput is
set by the working set, so reducing \emph{bytes touched} pays as well as
reducing \emph{patterns walked}.

\subsubsection*{Sharing one sphere among the moves of a codeword}

Lemma~\ref{lem:move} localises both the leaving and the entering set of a move,
and a closer reading shows that the leaving set depends on the moved
coordinate only through a membership predicate. Write $D(w)=\{j: w_j\neq c_j\}$
and let $c^{(p)}$ denote $c$ with coordinate $p$ replaced by $v$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:shared}
$w$ leaves the ball of $c$ under the move at $p$ if and only if $|D(w)|=R$ and
$p\notin D(w)$; and $w$ enters the ball of $c^{(p)}$ if and only if
$|D(w)|=R+1$, $p\in D(w)$ and $w_p=v$.
\end{lemma}

Consequently, putting
\[
T=\#\{w: |D(w)|=R,\ \mathrm{cnt}(w)=1\},\qquad
H_j=\#\{w: |D(w)|=R,\ \mathrm{cnt}(w)=1,\ j\in D(w)\},
\]
the number of words that the move at $p$ newly uncovers equals $T-H_p$ for
\emph{every} $p$ at once. A single enumeration of the full distance-$R$
sphere, of size $\binom{n}{R}(q-1)^R$, therefore replaces the $R+1$ separate
enumerations of $\binom{n-1}{R}(q-1)^R$ patterns each, a saving of
$(R+1)(n-R)/n$ (2.00, 2.50 and 2.78 on the three cells). The updates to $T$
and $H$ are executed only when $\mathrm{cnt}(w)=1$, which on the loose cells
this method targets happens for $0.4\%$ of words at $M=169$ and $0.07\%$ at
$M=216$ in $\Kq{6}{8}{4}$, so the inner loop reduces to one load and one
compare --- half the memory traffic per pattern of the original.

\subsubsection*{The entering side from the uncovered words}

The entering side does not share in the same way: enumerating the
distance-$(R+1)$ sphere once costs $(q-1)n/(R+1)^2$ times the per-position
walks, which is worse for these parameters. But the entering side counts only
words with $\mathrm{cnt}(w)=0$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:shared} membership of
the entering set is decided in $O(n)$ from the digits of the word. Maintaining
the set $U$ of uncovered words explicitly therefore replaces
$(R+1)\binom{n-1}{R}(q-1)^R$ counter reads by $O(n|U|)$ work, which is
profitable exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ulcond}
n\,|U| \;<\; (R+1)\binom{n-1}{R}(q-1)^R .
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is $5\,000$, $109\,375$ and $840\,350$ on the three cells
while the mean $|U|$ during a run is $58$, $30$ and ${\approx}10^4$, so
\eqref{eq:ulcond} holds by two to four orders of magnitude for the whole
useful life of the search. The solver tests \eqref{eq:ulcond} each iteration
and falls back to the sphere walk when it fails, so it is never worse.
$U$ is kept as an append-ordered array of (index, digit vector) pairs together
with a presence bitset over $\Zq^n$; commits append the words they uncover,
stale entries are removed by an amortised compaction, and no $q^n$-sized index
array is needed.

Together, the counter reads per candidate codeword fall from
$2(R+1)\binom{n-1}{R}(q-1)^R$ to $\binom{n}{R}(q-1)^R$, a factor of $4.00$,
$5.00$ and $5.56$.

\subsubsection*{A wider neighbourhood for free}

$T-H_p$ does not depend on the value $v$ written into coordinate $p$, and the
entering counts read off $U$ are naturally indexed by the pair $(p,v)$ at no
extra cost. One sphere enumeration plus one pass over $U$ therefore yields the
exact objective change of \emph{all} $n(q-1)$ single-coordinate moves of a
codeword, not only the $R+1$ that move it towards $u$. Since the parameter
study of the conference version found candidate breadth to be the dominant
tuning knob, widening the neighbourhood from $R+1$ to $n(q-1)$ moves per
codeword --- a factor of $7.5$ to $11$ here --- is the natural way to spend
the saving.

\subsubsection*{Narrower counters on the read path}

Coverage counters are $16$-bit because $M$ may exceed $255$, but evaluation
only ever asks whether a counter is $0$ or $1$. A side array
$\mathrm{st}(w)=\min(\mathrm{cnt}(w),2)$ carries everything the read path needs
in one byte or in two bits, while the exact counters stay for the write path;
the extra write costs nothing measurable because commits are under $1\%$ of the
time. On $\Kq{8}{9}{4}$ the two-bit form shrinks the read path from $268$\,MB
to $33.5$\,MB, bringing it inside the $117$\,MB last-level cache.

\subsubsection*{Correctness}

Every one of these changes is an implementation change: it computes the same
objective differences as the published solver and must therefore follow the
same search trajectory from the same seed. That is verified bit-for-bit over
24 configurations ($q$ from 2 to 7, $R=0$, $R=n-1$, sizes above and below the
optimum) $\times$ 9 variants, and separately the number of uncovered words the
solver reports is required to equal the number the standalone verifier
computes from the written code file --- including the runs that end at a
nonzero count.

session, so wall clock is meaningless and every figure below is the solver's
own \texttt{CLOCK\_PROCESS\_CPUTIME\_ID} reading, with the run order of the

variant was given a fixed iteration budget; since they all follow the same
trajectory this is a fully controlled comparison with no search-quality

After these changes the profile has moved: candidate evaluation falls to
$72.8\%$, $81.7\%$ and $98.2\%$ of the run time, and committing a move --- which
concerns a single $(p,v)$ pair and so cannot use the shared identity --- rises
to $19.2\%$, $14.5\%$ and $1.6\%$. It is the natural next target.

\subsubsection*{What did not help}

Several plausible ideas were implemented and measured, and did not work; we
record them because the reasons are structural rather than accidental.

\emph{Caching move evaluations between iterations} is impossible here. A
cached value for codeword $c$ survives a commit on $c'$ only if their
evaluation spheres are disjoint, which needs $d(c,c')>2R+2$; but $2R+2\ge n$
for every cell of interest, so every commit invalidates every entry. Large
$R$ relative to $n$ --- the defining feature of the cells whose bounds are
inherited arithmetic --- is exactly what rules caching out.

\emph{Early exit on a lower bound.} During a candidate's walk the final
change is bounded below using the fact that the entering count cannot exceed
$|U|$. The bound is exact and prunes $1.03\%$, $0.006\%$ and $0.000\%$ of
patterns on the three cells: it only becomes strong as $|U|$ falls, and small
$|U|$ is precisely what makes the sparse evaluator fast instead.

\emph{Inverting the leaving side}, i.e.\ maintaining a list of the singly
covered words as we do for the uncovered ones, pays only if
$n|U_1| < \binom{n}{R}(q-1)^R$. Measured on the recorded codes, $|U_1|$ is
5357, 6592 and ${\sim}10^6$ against thresholds of 417, 5469 and 33\,614: it
fails everywhere. Uncovered words are rare by construction, singly covered
words are not.

\emph{Symmetry reduction.} Translating the code so that one codeword sits at
$0^n$ and freezing it quotients out the $q^n$ translations, but gives no
measurable benefit ($4.87\times$ against $5.26\times$ on $\Kq{6}{8}{4}$,
$0.89\times$ against $2.11\times$ on $\Kq{6}{6}{3}$). A local search does not
revisit states exactly, so removing a symmetry removes almost no work; all it
does here is shrink the candidate pool by one codeword, which on the small
cell measurably hurts.

\emph{Deterministic focus on the longest-uncovered word}, the cheapest form
of a guided objective, is clearly harmful: $2.04\times$ against $5.26\times$
on $\Kq{6}{8}{4}$, and on $\Kq{6}{6}{3}$ only 5 of 20 runs reached the target
at all. Always choosing the same word destroys the randomisation that lets
focused local search leave a plateau. A stochastic age weighting remains
untested.

\emph{Software prefetching} and \emph{transparent huge pages} are both within
noise. For huge pages the reason is architectural: the base page on this host
is already 64\,kB, so even the 268\,MB counter array spans only 4096 pages.

\subsubsection*{Restart policy}

The empirical run-time distributions answer the restart question directly. At
targets the search reaches routinely the distribution is light-tailed
(coefficient of variation $0.20$--$0.47$ on the two loose cells) and a fixed
restart interval cannot help; calibrating the best observed gain against the
memoryless null gives $p=1.0$. On the tight cell $\Kq{6}{6}{3}$ at a target
hard enough that a quarter of runs stall, the distribution becomes heavier
than exponential and restarting every ${\approx}1.6$ CPU seconds cuts the
expected time to target by a factor of $4.2$ ($p=0.002$). The policy that
follows is to run one chain while it is still improving and to rely on
independent parallel workers --- which the campaign already uses --- rather
than on timed restarts.
