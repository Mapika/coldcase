## A. Where the time goes

The solver of the conference version is a focused local search: pick a random
uncovered word `u`, collect every move that would cover it (every codeword `c`
at Hamming distance `R+1` from `u`, with one coordinate `p` nudged from `c_p`
to `u_p`), evaluate them all exactly, commit the best. Evaluating one move
costs a walk over the `S = C(n-1,R)(q-1)^R` "sphere-difference" patterns of
Lemma 1, reading two coverage counters per pattern.

Instrumenting each phase with `CLOCK_THREAD_CPUTIME_ID` (build with `-DPROF`)
gives the following breakdown for one single-threaded worker.

| phase | `K_6(6,3)` `M=41` | `K_6(8,4)` `M=169` | `K_8(9,4)` `M=2944` |
|---|---|---|---|
| candidate evaluation | **92.94%** | **98.51%** | **99.62%** |
| commit | 3.30% | 0.84% | 0.37% |
| pick uncovered word | 0.82% | 0.55% | 0.00% |
| distance scan + candidate collection | 0.74% | 0.02% | 0.00% |
| best-move selection / tie break | 0.53% | 0.04% | 0.00% |
| copy of the incumbent, kicks | 0.00% | 0.00% | 0.00% |
| | | | |
| iterations measured | 20 000 | 2 000 | 120 |
| CPU seconds | 3.04 | 36.2 | 41.5 |
| sphere `S = C(n-1,R)(q-1)^R` | 1 250 | 21 875 | 168 070 |
| mean candidate moves / iteration | 58.1 | 136.2 | 278.5 |
| mean uncovered words during the run | 58 | 30 | 9 475 |
| ns per evaluated pattern | 1.95 | 5.99 | 7.35 |
| counter array | 93 kB (L2) | 3.4 MB (L3) | 268 MB (DRAM) |

Candidate evaluation is 93–99.6% of the run time on all three cells, and its
share *grows* with instance size. Everything else — choosing the uncovered
word, computing the `M` Hamming distances, the tie-break scan, the commit —
is together under 7% on the smallest cell and under 0.4% on the largest.
Optimising anything except candidate evaluation is therefore pointless, and
this is the single most useful thing the profile says.

Two secondary observations:

* The cost per evaluated pattern rises from 1.9 ns on `K_6(6,3)`
  (`q^n·2 B = 93 kB`, resident in L2) to 6.0 ns on `K_6(8,4)` (3.4 MB, L3)
  to 7.4 ns on `K_8(9,4)` (268 MB, DRAM). The walk is a strided gather, so
  the working-set size, not the instruction count, sets the throughput. Any
  reduction in *bytes touched* therefore pays at least as well as a
  reduction in *patterns walked*.
* `pick_uncovered` degenerates to a linear scan of `q^n` once uncovered words
  are rarer than 1/64 of the space. This sounds alarming and is not: the scan
  stops at the first uncovered word, so its expected cost is `q^n/|U|` reads,
  which stays below 1% of an iteration for every instance we measured.

## B. Sharing one sphere between the moves of a codeword

The `R+1` candidate moves generated by a single codeword `c` (one per
coordinate where `c` and `u` differ) were evaluated by `R+1` independent
sphere walks. They need not be.

**Lemma (shared sphere).** Let `c` be a codeword, `p` a coordinate with
`c_p != v`, and `c'` the codeword with `c'_p = v` and `c'_j = c_j` otherwise.
Write `D(w) = {j : w_j != c_j}`. Then

* `w` leaves the ball of `c` iff `|D(w)| = R` and `p ∉ D(w)`;
* `w` enters the ball of `c'` iff `|D(w)| = R+1`, `p ∈ D(w)` and `w_p = v`.

*Proof.* `d(w,c') = d(w,c) + [w_p = c_p] - [w_p = v]`, so leaving requires
`d(w,c) = R` and `w_p = c_p`, i.e. `p ∉ D(w)`, and entering requires
`d(w,c') = R` with `w_p = v != c_p`, i.e. `p ∈ D(w)` and `|D(w)| = R+1`. ∎

The leaving condition depends on `p` only through the single predicate
`p ∉ D(w)`. Hence, defining over the *whole* distance-`R` sphere of `c`

  `T   = #{ w : |D(w)| = R, cnt(w) = 1 }`,
  `H_j = #{ w : |D(w)| = R, cnt(w) = 1, j ∈ D(w) }`,

the number of words that the move at `p` would newly uncover is
`T − H_p`, **for every `p` simultaneously**. One enumeration of
`C(n,R)(q-1)^R` words replaces `R+1` enumerations of `C(n-1,R)(q-1)^R`
words, a saving of

  `(R+1)(n−R)/n` = 2.00 for `K_6(6,3)`, 2.50 for `K_6(8,4)`, 2.78 for `K_8(9,4)`.

The bookkeeping of `T` and `H` is executed only when `cnt(w) = 1`. Measured
on the recorded codes (`cnthist.py`), the fraction of words with `cnt(w)=1`
is 11.5% on `K_6(6,3)` at `M=41` (mean multiplicity 2.55) but only 0.39% on
`K_6(8,4)` at `M=169` (mean 5.18) and 0.073% at `M=216` (mean 6.62). So on
the loose, high-redundancy cells this project targets — which are precisely
the cells whose upper bounds are inherited arithmetic — the inner loop is one
load, one compare and a branch taken well under 1% of the time. Crucially it
now touches **one** counter per pattern instead of two.

## C. The entering side from the uncovered-word list

The entering side does not share in the same way — enumerating the
distance-`(R+1)` sphere once costs `(q-1)n/(R+1)^2` times the per-position
walks, which is *worse* for our parameters. But the entering side counts only
words with `cnt(w) = 0`, and by the Lemma the test for membership is
`|D(x)| = R+1 ∧ x_p = u_p`, decidable in `O(n)` from the digits of `x`.

So if the set `U` of uncovered words is maintained explicitly, `neg_p` for
all `p` at once costs `O(n|U|)` per codeword rather than
`(R+1)C(n-1,R)(q-1)^R` counter reads. This is profitable whenever

  `n|U| < (R+1) C(n-1,R) (q-1)^R`,

i.e. `|U| < 833` on `K_6(6,3)`, `< 13672` on `K_6(8,4)`, `< 93372` on
`K_8(9,4)` — thresholds that the search is below for essentially its whole
useful life (the measured mean `|U|` during a run is 58, 30 and ~10⁴
respectively). The implementation tests the inequality every iteration and
falls back to the sphere walk when it fails, so the evaluator is never worse
than the per-position one.

`U` is maintained as an append-ordered array of (index, digit vector) pairs
plus a "present in the array" bitset over `Z_q^n` (`q^n/8` bytes). A commit
appends every word whose counter it drove to zero; entries whose counter has
since risen again are dropped by a compaction triggered when the array
exceeds `2|U| + 64` entries. No deletion by index is ever needed, so no
`q^n`-sized index array is required. If the array overflows its cap
(2²⁰ entries) the list is marked invalid and rebuilt by one linear scan when
`|U|` next drops below half the cap.

One caveat on applicability: the shared enumeration amortises over the `R+1`
moves of one codeword, so it assumes all of them are evaluated. That is the
shipped configuration (`--cand` unlimited, which the conference version'
parameter study already selected), but under aggressive candidate sampling
the shared evaluator would do work for moves it then discards and could be
slower than the per-move walk.

Combining §B and §C, the counter reads per iteration fall from
`2(R+1)C(n-1,R)(q-1)^R` to `C(n,R)(q-1)^R` per candidate codeword, a factor

  4.00 (`K_6(6,3)`), 5.00 (`K_6(8,4)`), 5.56 (`K_8(9,4)`).

## C2. A free consequence: the whole 1-move neighbourhood

Two facts about §B and §C deserve to be put together. The leaving count
`T − H_p` does not depend on the *value* `v` the move writes into coordinate
`p` at all; and the entering count read off the uncovered-word list is
naturally indexed by the pair `(p, v)` — incrementing `neg[j][x_j]` costs
exactly what incrementing `neg[j]` cost. Therefore

> one enumeration of the distance-`R` sphere of `c`, plus one pass over the
> uncovered words, yields the exact delta of **all `n(q−1)` single-coordinate
> moves of `c`**, not only the `R+1` that move it towards `u`.

The candidate set can thus be widened from `R+1` to `n(q−1)` moves per
codeword — 30, 40 and 56 instead of 4, 5 and 5 on our three cells, a 7.5x to
11x wider neighbourhood — at no evaluation cost. Given that the conference
version's parameter study found *candidate breadth to be the dominant
tuning knob*, this is the natural thing to spend the saving on. `--wide`
does it; it requires the uncovered list, because the fallback entering side
would have to walk `n(q−1)` spheres instead of `R+1`.

## D. Narrower counters for the read path

`cnt` is `uint16` because `M` can exceed 255. But evaluation only ever asks
whether a counter is 0 or 1. A side array `st(w) = min(cnt(w), 2)` therefore
carries all the information the read path needs in one byte (`--st 1`) or two
bits (`--st 2`), while `cnt` stays exact for the write path. The commit
updates `st` only when the new count is ≤ 2, which costs nothing measurable
because commits are under 1% of the run time.

## E. Symmetry reduction

`Z_q^n` is homogeneous under translation `w ↦ w − t`, which is an isometry,
so any code may be translated to put one codeword at `0^n` without changing
its covering radius or its uncovered count. `--fix0` applies that translation
once at start-up and then freezes codeword 0, quotienting out the `q^n`
translations. Coordinate permutations and per-coordinate value permutations
are also symmetries, but they cannot be fixed as cheaply (canonicalising
under them is itself a search), and they do not commute with the incremental
counter representation, so only the translation subgroup was exploited.

## F. Experimental protocol

The host is shared with a production sweep that keeps all 64 cores busy at
`nice 10` (104 workers x 4 OpenMP threads, load average > 400). Our runs are
capped at 8 concurrent single-threaded processes (`OMP_NUM_THREADS=1`) at
`nice 15`, i.e. at most 8 cores of work; in practice they were scheduled
about 0.5 cores in aggregate. **Wall clock therefore measures the sweep, not
the solver, and is not used.** The metric is the solver's own
`CLOCK_PROCESS_CPUTIME_ID` reading, which counts only cycles retired on
behalf of the measured process. Run order is randomised over the whole job
list so that any drift in background load is spread over variants rather than
confounded with them.

Two experiments:

1. **Fixed-iteration micro-benchmark.** Every implementation variant of
   §B–§D computes exactly the same move deltas as the original solver, hence
   follows exactly the same search trajectory from the same seed. This is
   verified bit-for-bit (§H, item 0). At a fixed `--iters` budget the two
   therefore do *identical work* and differ only in speed, which removes the
   search-quality confound entirely. 3 seeds × 3 repetitions per cell.
2. **Time-to-target.** Each cell starts from a *fixed* code file, so every
   run of every variant begins in the identical state and the seed drives
   only the search. 20 seeds per configuration, a fixed CPU budget per run,
   and a fixed target uncovered count; the solver records the CPU time and
   iteration at which `best_uncovered` first reached the target. Runs that
   never reach it are censored and reported as such.

| cell | `q^n` | `M` | start `|U|` | target `|U|` | budget (CPU s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `K_6(6,3)` | 46 656 | 41 | 1411 | 30 | 45 |
| `K_6(8,4)` | 1 679 616 | 169 | 854 | 20 | 90 |
| `K_8(9,4)` | 134 217 728 | 2944 | 11 640 | 10 800 | 100 |

Targets were calibrated from single long traces so that the reference
implementation reaches them in of the order of 10 CPU seconds. A first pass
used target 10 on `K_6(6,3)` and censored 25% of the runs
(`results_ttt_target10.csv` is kept); the reported run uses 30.

Ratios of medians are reported with a percentile-bootstrap 95% confidence
interval (10 000 resamples), and with the rank-based common-language effect
size `P(X_variant < X_base)`.

Reading the table:

* The two structural changes carry the result. Sharing the sphere (§B) is
  worth 1.32x/1.95x/1.95x on top of the micro-optimised walk, and reading the
  entering side off the uncovered-word list (§C) takes it to
  2.21x/5.57x/6.06x. The analytical prediction from counter reads alone was

  because its counter array is L2-resident, so the walk is limited by
  instruction throughput rather than by memory, and the `cnt(w)=1` branch is
  taken 11.5% of the time there (§B).

  set spills. `uint8` beats `uint16` by 1.65x on the two larger cells and

  (0.82x) and wins biggest on the largest (2.44x alone, and 9.86x combined),
  where it shrinks the read path from 268 MB to 33.5 MB and brings it inside
  the 117 MB L3.
* Huge pages and software prefetch do nothing (within noise) on this host,
  for the reasons in §H.4.

Re-profiling `p5` from the same fixed starts shows the bottleneck has moved:

|---|---|---|---|

| pick uncovered word | 0.8% → 1.7% | 0.9% → 2.7% | 0.0% → 0.0% |

(before → after). The commit is now a sixth of the run time on the two
smaller cells, and it is the obvious next target: it still walks `2S`

### G.4 Restart and portfolio policy

The empirical run-time distributions were used to ask whether a fixed restart
interval beats running one chain to the budget. Because
`min_t E(t) <= E(budget)` holds by construction, the apparent gain is
calibrated against the same procedure applied to simulated exponential
(memoryless) run times with the same mean, for which restarts are truly
neutral.

| cell | target | cv (reference) | cv (p5) | best gain | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `K_6(6,3)` | 30 | 1.25 | 1.22 | 1.29x | 0.30 |
| `K_6(8,4)` | 20 | 0.47 | 0.44 | 1.00x | 1.00 |
| `K_8(9,4)` | 10 800 | 0.20 | 0.30 | 1.00x | 1.00 |
| `K_6(6,3)` | **10** (harder) | 1.00 | 1.06 | **2.56x / 4.19x** | **0.026 / 0.002** |

The conclusion is target-dependent and worth stating that way. At targets the
search reaches routinely, the run-time distribution is *light*-tailed
(cv 0.2–0.5 on the two loose cells) and restarts are provably useless — they
can only throw away progress. On the tight cell at a hard target, where a
quarter of the runs stall, the distribution becomes heavier than exponential
and restarting every ~1.6 CPU s cuts the expected time to target by a factor
of 4. Practical policy: **do not restart while the search is still improving;
restart only on the tight, low-redundancy cells and only once a target is
being missed by a noticeable fraction of runs** — which is also exactly the
regime where independent parallel workers (the campaign's existing design)
buy the same thing for free.





## H. What did not work, and what was not tried

**0. The correctness gate (not a negative result, but the reason to trust the
rest).** Every implementation variant of §B–§D computes the same move deltas
as the published solver, so from the same seed it must follow the same search
trajectory and end at the same uncovered count. `selftest.sh` checks exactly
that over 8 configurations (`q` from 2 to 7, `R = 0`, `R = n-1`, `M` above and
below the optimum) x 3 seeds x 9 variants, and separately checks that the
number the solver reports equals the number `cov/verify_cov.py` computes from
the written code file — including when that number is not zero.

Result: **24 configurations x 9 variants, all bit-identical**, and **70
solver/verifier cross-checks, all equal** — including the runs that end at
63, 199, 117, 105, 93, 89, 83, 59 and 6 uncovered words, not only the ones
that end at 0. (`selftest.log`.) The verifier is `cov/verify_cov.py --method
both`, i.e. two independent exhaustive methods, and it re-reads the code from
disk; the solver's incremental counters are never the evidence.

**1. Inverting the *leaving* side of the evaluation** (maintaining a list of
the words with `cnt(w)=1`, exactly as §C does for `cnt(w)=0`, and testing
sphere membership in `O(n)` instead of walking). Analysed and rejected
without implementing, on measured data. It pays iff
`n |U_1| < C(n,R)(q-1)^R`, i.e. `|U_1| < 417` on `K_6(6,3)`, `< 5469` on
`K_6(8,4)`, `< 33614` on `K_8(9,4)`. The measured `|U_1|` on recorded codes
is 5357, 6592 and (extrapolating from the mean multiplicity) ~10^6. The
inequality fails everywhere except `K_6(8,4)` at the loosest sizes
(`|U_1| = 1231` at `M = 216`, where it would pay a further 4.4x) — and there
only once the search has converged, not during it. The asymmetry between the
two sides is real and worth stating: uncovered words are rare *by
construction* (the search is minimising them), singly-covered words are not.

**2. Caching move deltas between iterations.** Impossible in this regime, for
a structural reason worth recording. A cached delta for codeword `c` survives
a commit on codeword `c'` only if the two evaluation spheres are disjoint,
which requires `d(c,c') > 2R+2`. But `2R+2 >= n` for every cell of interest
(`8 > 6`, `10 > 8`, `10 > 9`), so no pair of codewords is ever far enough
apart and every commit invalidates every cache entry. High `R` relative to
`n` — the defining feature of the cells whose bounds are inherited
arithmetic — is exactly what rules out incremental caching.

**3. Early exit on a lower bound.** During a candidate's walk one can bound
the final delta below by `pos - neg - min(rem, |U| - neg)`, since the
entering count can never exceed the number of uncovered words. It is exact
and it prunes essentially nothing: `pos` grows at the `cnt(w)=1` rate, which
in the high-redundancy regime is well under 1% of patterns, so the bound
needs almost the whole walk before it can exceed the `|U|` slack.

Measured directly as a pruning rate with the `-DPROF` build (`--preset p2
--early`, which is the per-candidate evaluator the flag applies to): the
fraction of sphere patterns skipped is **1.03%** on `K_6(6,3)`, **0.006%** on
`K_6(8,4)` and **0.000%** on `K_8(9,4)`. It is not worth the branch. The
ordering matters here: the bound gets stronger as `|U|` shrinks, but `|U|`
shrinking is also what makes the sparse evaluator of §C fast, and the sparse
evaluator wins by orders of magnitude.

**4. Software prefetching** the first words of the next position subset, and
**transparent huge pages** for the counter arrays. Both are near no-ops on
this host, and for hugepages the reason is architectural: the aarch64 base
page here is already **64 kB**, so even the 268 MB counter array of
`K_8(9,4)` spans only 4096 pages and the TLB is not the bottleneck. (THP is
set to `madvise` on this host, so `--huge` really is the difference between
huge pages and none.) On an x86-64 host with 4 kB pages the same array would
span 65 536 pages and the conclusion could differ.

Measured: `p5 + prefetch` is 2.20x / 5.54x / 6.00x against `p5`'s
2.20x / 5.41x / 6.11x -- indistinguishable. The `q-1` loads of the innermost
group are already independent, so the out-of-order engine has them in flight
without help. Huge pages: `p5` (with) 2.20x / 5.41x / 6.11x versus `p4`
(without) 2.21x / 5.57x / 6.06x -- also indistinguishable.

**5. Two-bit packed state array** (`min(cnt,2)` at 4 words per byte).

Not a negative result, but a strongly size-dependent one, which is why it is
not simply switched on: 2-bit packing **loses** on the smallest cell
(0.82x against the reference, versus 1.28x for the uint8 form) and **wins
biggest on the largest** (2.44x alone, and 9.86x once combined with the
shared sphere and the uncovered list, against 6.11x for the uint8 form). The
crossover is where the read path stops fitting in cache. `--st 2` should be
the default for `q^n` above a few million and `--st 1` below it.

**6. Caching the uncovered words for `pick_uncovered`** (`--ucache`; the fix
that helped the CUDA port). Here it is actively harmful, and the profile says
why: the scan it avoids costs `q^n/|U|` reads, under 1% of an iteration, while
the cached buffer is filled by a *contiguous* scan and therefore focuses the
search on one region of `Z_q^n`. On `K_8(9,4)` it even damages the greedy
initialisation, which uses the same routine: initial uncovered 60 422 with the
cache versus 10 954 without.

Measured with the fixed-start protocol, which excludes the initialisation
effect: on `K_6(8,4)` the paired ratio is 6.02x [3.45, 7.31] against p5's
5.26x [4.91, 5.75], and on `K_6(6,3)` 1.51x [0.57, 3.81] against 2.11x
[2.05, 2.19]. The intervals overlap the baseline variant in both directions
and the spread is enormous (0.06x to 69.8x on the small cell), so there is no
evidence of a real effect during the search -- while the initialisation
damage is unambiguous. Verdict: leave it off.

**7. Symmetry reduction** (`--fix0`).

No measurable benefit: paired ratio 4.87x [3.00, 7.51] on `K_6(8,4)` against
p5's 5.26x, and 0.89x [0.50, 1.84] on `K_6(6,3)` against 2.11x; median
iterations to target 702 vs 676 and 20 628 vs 8 736. The translation subgroup
has order `q^n`, which sounds like a lot, but a local search never revisits a
state exactly, so quotienting by it removes almost no work; all it really does
is remove one codeword from the candidate pool, which on the small cell is
1 of 41 and measurably hurts. Symmetry reduction is the right tool for exact
/ exhaustive methods, not for this one.

**8. Longest-uncovered-first focus** (`--upick`), the cheapest form of a
guided/weighted objective, available for free once §C maintains `U` in
insertion order.

Clearly harmful, and by a lot: paired ratio 2.04x [1.34, 3.54] on `K_6(8,4)`
against p5's 5.26x (median 1 988 iterations to target instead of 676), and on
`K_6(6,3)` only 5 of 20 runs reached the target at all, with a paired ratio
of 0.04x on those. Deterministically focusing on the most persistent
uncovered word destroys the randomisation that lets focused local search
escape a plateau: the same word is picked again and again and the same small
set of moves is re-evaluated. A *stochastic* weighting (sample proportional
to age rather than take the maximum) is the version worth trying next; this
experiment only rules out the deterministic form.

**9. Not attempted.** (a) SVE gather loads for the sphere walk: the walk is a
strided gather with a stride of `q^(n-1-p)` words, and on Neoverse-V2 a
gather is executed as one load per element, so it cannot beat the scalar
loop, which already has all `q-1` loads of the innermost group in flight.
(b) Saturating 8-bit `cnt` with periodic exact recount, which would halve the
write-path footprint as well: correct but it puts a soundness-critical
invariant (no counter ever exceeded 255 unnoticed) in the hot path, and the
write path is under 1% of the time. (c) Multi-thread scaling: the host was
saturated by the production sweep for the whole session, so any parallel
efficiency number would be measuring the sweep. The parallel unit changes
from "one candidate move" to "one candidate codeword", which reduces the
number of tasks by `R+1` (to 15–56 here) while making each `R+1` times
larger; that is still ample for 4–8 threads but should be re-measured on an
idle machine.

## I. Recommendation

**Use `covsearch2 --preset p5b --wide` on loose cells (`q^n` large, redundancy
`M|B_R|/q^n` above about 4), and `--preset p5` on small tight cells.**
The single safest default is `--preset p5b`, which is 2.03x / 6.12x / 8.07x
faster than the current production solver on the three benchmark cells with
a *bit-identical* search, so it can be substituted with no change in the
sequence of codes visited. `--wide` adds a real search improvement on the
loose cells (10.97x and 8.38x end-to-end on `K_6(8,4)` and `K_8(9,4)`) but
costs a factor 3.6 on the tight `K_6(6,3)`, so it is a per-cell decision, not
a default.

The acceptance criterion set for this work — at least 1.5x median time to
target on at least two cells with identical correctness — is met with a wide
margin on all three cells, and the correctness half is established twice:
bit-identical trajectories on 24 configurations, and agreement with the
project's exhaustive verifiers (`verify_cov.py` pure + numpy,
`verify_independent.py` mindist + dilate) on every code written, including a
`K_6(8,4) <= 169` code produced by `p5b` and by `p5b --wide` that all four
methods confirm.

### Caveats

1. **The host was shared throughout.** A production sweep ran at `nice 10`
   for the whole session, between 6 and 156 concurrent workers. All timings
   are process CPU time, run order is randomised, and the headline numbers
   are *paired per-seed ratios* on trajectory-identical runs, which is the
   strongest control available short of an idle machine. Absolute CPU seconds
   are not comparable between sessions and should not be quoted.
2. **Three cells is three cells.** The mechanism (§B, §C) predicts the
   speedup from `n, R, q` and `|U|` alone, and the measurements land where the
   prediction says they should, but the parameter range covered is
   `q ∈ {6,8}`, `n ∈ {6,8,9}`, `R ∈ {3,4}`. Cells with small `R` relative to
   `n`, where `|B_R|` is small and the sphere walk is short, are not
   represented and the constant factors there will differ.
3. **`--wide` is measured on two cells, and it hurts on the third.** Its
   benefit should be re-checked per cell before being switched on for a
   production descent.
4. **Nothing here is multi-threaded evidence.** All measurements are
   single-threaded. The parallel unit changes from "one candidate move" to
   "one candidate codeword", which is `R+1` times fewer tasks (15–57 here);
   that should still fill 4–8 threads but was not measurable on this host.
5. **The memory footprint grew.** `--st 1` adds `q^n` bytes, `--st 2` adds
   `q^n/4`, and `--eval 2` adds `q^n/8` for the presence bitset plus up to
   `2^20 (8+n)` bytes for the list. For `K_8(9,4)` that is 268 MB → 419 MB
   with `--st 1`, or 318 MB with `--st 2`. On the very largest instances the
   project cares about (`q^n ≈ 10^9`, as in the running sweep) this matters
   and `--st 2` is the right choice.
6. **The uncovered-word list has a cap** (`2^20` entries). Above it the
   solver silently falls back to the sphere walk — correct, but with the old
   performance. Instances whose uncovered count stays above a million for
   long will see the §C speedup only late in the run.
