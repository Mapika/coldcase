\subsection{Host-coherent memory: past the accelerator-memory
wall}\label{sec:gpu:hc}

The transform engine of Section~\ref{sec:gpu} stores $R+1$ layer arrays of
$32$-bit counters and one $16$-bit multiplicity array, $4(R{+}1)+2$ bytes
per word of $\Zq^n$. With every array resident in the $96$\,GB of GPU
memory (HBM), the largest searchable cells have $q^n\approx 3.5\cdot 10^9$
--- which is precisely where K\'eri's tables keep several of their loosest
entries: $\Kq{10}{10}{5}$ ($q^n=10^{10}$, upper bound $7106$ against a
sphere-covering bound of $612$) and its neighbours sit one order of
magnitude beyond the wall.

The machine used here (NVIDIA GH200) pairs the GPU with $480$\,GB of
CPU-attached LPDDR memory behind a cache-coherent interconnect: a GPU
thread can dereference any CPU pointer, with native atomics, at
interconnect rather than PCIe cost. We exploit this by \emph{placing each
array independently}. The layer arrays, touched only by the streaming axis
passes of Proposition~\ref{prop:dct}, move to LPDDR; the multiplicity
array $\mathrm{cnt}$, hammered by the random-access ball walks of the
incremental updates and candidate evaluations, stays in HBM. For
$\Kq{10}{10}{5}$ this is $240$\,GB of LPDDR and $21$\,GB of HBM; the whole
of K\'eri's $q\le 10$ tables becomes memory-feasible on one node.

Two implementation findings matter for reproduction. First, allocating
the LPDDR arrays with the unified-memory allocator and a
preferred-location hint is a factor $\sim\!30$ slower than plain
\texttt{malloc} accessed through the coherent page tables: the fault-based
migration machinery is pure overhead for arrays that will never fit in
HBM. Second, and less obviously, plain \texttt{malloc} is not stable
either: under the default kernel policy the GPU's access counters
\emph{migrate} GPU-hot pages into HBM, which silently converts a
``host-memory'' benchmark into an HBM one --- until the working set
exceeds HBM, at which point the engine thrashes ($86$\,GB of layers
filled HBM to $96.8/97.9$\,GB and stalled a $22$-second transform past a
ten-minute timeout). Binding the allocations to the CPU NUMA node
(\texttt{mbind}/\texttt{MPOL\_BIND}) forbids the migration and makes the
placement deterministic. All numbers below use the pinned configuration;
outputs were verified bit-identical across all placements.

\subsection{Kernel-level optimizations}\label{sec:gpu:kernels}

Two kernel changes, ported from an experimental sibling engine after
profiling showed the ball-walk kernels compute-bound on per-word
radix arithmetic and launch-limited on small batches, compound across
every search:

\emph{Launch geometry.} The original engine walked one codeword's ball
with a single thread block; a single placement on a $\Kq{10}{10}{5}$-sized
ball ($1.6\cdot 10^7$ words) used $0.4\%$ of the GPU. Splitting each walk
over up to $8192$ blocks makes single-word updates --- the innermost
operation of the exact lazy-greedy rebuild --- $30\times$ faster on
$\Kq{8}{9}{4}$ ($0.80\to 0.027$\,ms) and $37\times$ faster on
$\Kq{8}{10}{4}$ ($1.34\to 0.036$\,ms), and batched candidate evaluations
($64$ at a time) $3.7$--$4.3\times$ faster; a complete exact-greedy cover
construction on $\Kq{8}{9}{4}$ drops from $40.4$ to $28.7$\,s
($1.4\times$ end to end, identical cover size). The change is pure
scheduling; the arithmetic is untouched.

\emph{Precomputed ball-offset tables.} Ball walks are driven by a table of
packed coordinate-shift patterns; each visited word costs one table load
and at most $R$ shared-memory adds, with no division or modulo in the hot
loop. An alternative table format (position mask plus per-position offset
nibbles, walked entirely in registers) was ported and paired-benchmarked;
it performs within measurement noise of the packed-byte format on all
cells tried ($0.027/0.036$\,ms single-word updates on both formats), and
the engine retains both behind a switch.

A third transplanted idea --- tagging each word's last coverer during the
mark pass so that per-codeword losses fall out of one linear scan
(``owner trick''), replacing one full transform --- is reported for
completeness as a \emph{negative} result in this engine: the transform
computes all losses in $17$\,ms against the owner pass's $28$\,ms on
$\Kq{8}{9}{4}$, and $145$ against $669$\,ms on $\Kq{8}{10}{4}$, because
the mark pass must re-walk every codeword's ball ($M\cdot|B_R|$ atomics)
where the transform streams $O(nRq^n)$ words. The owner pass wins only
where $M\cdot|B_R|\ll nRq^n$, a regime our record cells do not enter.
It remains available for the sparse-solution problems of
Section~\ref{sec:gpu:framework}.

\subsection{What dense local search cannot do: private-uniform
incumbents}\label{sec:gpu:rigid}

The engine's failures delimit the method as sharply as its records. The
K\'eri upper bound $\Kq{8}{10}{4}\le 11776$ is a direct sum
$23\times 512$: a $\Kq{8}{4}{2}$-code of size $23$ summed with a
$\Kq{8}{6}{2}$-code of size $512$. Product structure makes the incumbent
\emph{private-uniform}: every codeword privately covers $\approx 9800$
words, an order of magnitude above the sphere-packing average, so the
deletion of any $k$ codewords instantly owes $\sim 9800k$ words that a
same-size rebuild must repay exactly. In a dedicated eight-hour siege
with deficit-aware ruins (removing up to $512$ codewords beyond the
notch target so that the rebuild optimizes over genuine slack, $2794$
rounds, $1.17\cdot 10^6$ exact-greedy placements), the full notch ladder
failed: at deficits $32/16/8/4/2$ the best residuals after $40$-minute
budgets were $827296$, $88959$, $327344$, $410006$ and $402717$ uncovered
words, and at deficit \emph{one} --- a single codeword below the
incumbent --- three attempts with budgets escalating to $2.6$ hours ended
at best $77229$ uncovered.
The same holds for $\Kq{9}{10}{4}=27\times 729$. We conclude that
word-level ruin-and-recreate, however exact its evaluations, cannot
un-knit a product construction whose loss landscape is flat and high
everywhere; progress on these cells requires moves in the factor spaces
(replacing the $\Kq{8}{4}{2}$ factor of size $23$ by one of size $22$,
if it exists, would give $\Kq{8}{10}{4}\le 11264$ outright) or seeds
with genuine slack. By contrast, the loose high-redundancy incumbents
of the same tables (ratio to the sphere-covering bound above
$\approx 8$) fall to the exact greedy constructor alone
(Section~\ref{sec:gpu}); the boundary between the two regimes is the
practical content of this subsection.

\subsection{A problem-agnostic core}\label{sec:gpu:framework}

Nothing in the engine below the transform is specific to covering codes.
The state core (dense fields over a product space
$\prod_j \mathbb{Z}_{m_j}$ with per-array HBM/LPDDR placement, exact
incremental updates and gathers over precomputed support tables,
reductions and extraction) and the search core (exact lazy greedy,
ruin-and-recreate, verification discipline) interact with the problem
only through a four-entry oracle: from an indicator field, produce the
coverage multiplicity field; from the uncovered set, the exact gain
field; from the singly-covered set, the exact loss field; and walk one
solution element's support set. A problem plugs in by supplying
\begin{itemize}
\item the axis sizes $m_1,\dots,m_n$ of its product space;
\item a separable per-axis transform pass computing ``number of
  $S$-elements whose neighbourhood covers $x$'' for all $x$ at once;
\item a host-built table enumerating one element's support set as
  coordinate shifts.
\end{itemize}
The $q$-ary Hamming transform of Proposition~\ref{prop:dct} (in mixed-radix
generality) is plugin one. As an existence proof of the seams, a second
plugin --- dominating sets on torus grids under Chebyshev (king-move)
balls, whose transform is a circular window sum with a \emph{single}
layer --- was implemented in an afternoon, validated exhaustively against
brute force, and driven by the unchanged search core to a $9$-vertex
dominating set of the $9\times 9$ king torus, matching the known optimum
$\lceil 9/3\rceil^2$. A systematic record hunt over torus domination
tables, and the framework itself, are deferred to follow-up work; we
flag here only that the memory model of Section~\ref{sec:gpu:hc} and the
exactness discipline transfer unchanged.
